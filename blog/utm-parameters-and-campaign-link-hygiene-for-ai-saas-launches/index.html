UTM Parameters and Campaign Link Hygiene for AI SaaS Launches

You post a launch thread, send a newsletter, run a small ad test and get a podcast mention in the same week. Then signups jump and you cannot say which of them caused it. Or worse, your analytics shows half your traffic as "direct" because links were untagged. UTM parameters fix attribution, but sloppy ones create their own mess: fragmented reports, duplicate URLs in search, and misleading data. This guide covers a small set of rules that keeps campaign tracking clean for a young AI/SaaS site.

1. What UTM parameters do, and the five you need

UTMs are query-string tags appended to a link so your analytics tool can record where a visit came from. The standard five are:

  • utm_source: the specific origin, such as newsletter-name, producthunt, linkedin.
  • utm_medium: the channel type, such as email, social, cpc, referral.
  • utm_campaign: the initiative, such as launch-2026-q1.
  • utm_content: optional, to tell two links in the same placement apart (for example header-link versus footer-link).
  • utm_term: optional, mainly for paid search keywords.

Source, medium and campaign are the minimum. A link with only a source produces reports that are hard to group.

2. Never tag your own internal links

This is the most common and most damaging mistake. If a banner on your home page links to /pricing?utm_source=homepage&utm_medium=banner, then every click starts a new session, overwrites the original source of that visitor, and credits "homepage" for conversions that really came from search or a newsletter. UTMs are for links on other sites, emails and ads that point to you. For internal clicks, use event tracking (a click event with a label) instead of changing the URL.

3. Keep tagged URLs out of search results

Tagged links can get shared, copied and linked to from other sites, so Google may find many URLs that are the same page with different query strings. Protect yourself in three ways:

  1. Self-referencing canonical. Every page should declare its clean URL, for example <link rel="canonical" href="https://example.com/pricing">, with no parameters. Then /pricing?utm_source=x consolidates to /pricing.
  2. Do not block parameters in robots.txt. If Google cannot crawl the tagged URL it cannot see the canonical tag. Let it crawl, and let the canonical do the work.
  3. Strip parameters from sitemaps and internal links. Only clean canonical URLs belong there.

Check Search Console's page indexing report for URLs containing utm_. If any are indexed as separate pages, your canonical tags are missing or being ignored.

4. Write a naming convention and stick to it

Analytics tools treat LinkedIn, linkedin and linked-in as three different sources. Pick rules once:

  • All lowercase, hyphens instead of spaces, no special characters.
  • A fixed list of allowed mediums (for example email, social, cpc, referral, partner). Do not invent new ones per campaign.
  • A campaign name pattern such as topic-yyyy-mm, so launch-2026-11 sorts and groups well.
  • No personal data in any parameter. Never put an email address, name or user ID in a UTM, since URLs are logged, shared and stored in third-party systems.

One caution on mediums: Google Analytics 4 groups traffic into default channels using specific medium and source values. A medium of email lands in the Email channel, cpc in Paid Search, but a made-up medium such as newsletterpush often falls into "Unassigned". Using the standard values avoids that.

5. Use a shared tracking sheet

Keep a simple spreadsheet with columns: date, channel, destination URL, source, medium, campaign, content, final tagged link, and owner. Build the final link with a formula that concatenates the pieces so nobody types parameters by hand. Before you publish a link anywhere, check that it is in the sheet. This takes a minute and removes most typos and duplicates, and months later it answers the question "what did we actually run?"

6. Handle redirects and short links carefully

Link shorteners and redirects can strip query strings, and some do it silently. After creating a short link, click it in a private window and confirm the final URL still carries the parameters and that your analytics records the session with the correct source. Also check that the landing page does not redirect through a login or regional page that drops the query string. If it does, preserve the parameters in the redirect rule or capture them on first load and store them for the signup form.

7. Carry attribution through to signup

A visit that is tagged but converts three days later, after a few untagged visits, will be credited to "direct" in last-click reports. To keep the original source, save the UTM values on first arrival in a first-party cookie or local storage, with an appropriate retention period and consent where your region requires it. Write them to hidden fields in your signup form so your CRM or product database records the first-touch source next to each new account. Your own data is more reliable than any third-party report. Make sure your privacy policy and cookie notice reflect what you store.

8. Know what UTMs cannot see

  • Untagged shares and dark social. Links pasted into private chats, Slack and many apps arrive without a referrer and show up as direct.
  • AI assistants. Some send a referrer, some strip it. Do not assume that "direct" is only people typing your URL.
  • Organic search. Never tag organic links; Google already reports them, and tagging them can disrupt that data.

Treat attribution as directional. Pair it with a simple "How did you hear about us?" free-text field on signup, which often reveals podcasts, communities and word of mouth that no parameter captures.

9. Review monthly

Once a month, open your analytics acquisition report and look for: sources with inconsistent capitalization, a large Unassigned bucket, campaigns with no medium, and internal referrals from your own domain. Fix the naming rules or the offending links, and note the date so you can tell later when the data changed.

Quick checklist

  • Source, medium and campaign set on every external link; content and term only when useful.
  • No UTMs on internal links; use click events instead.
  • Self-referencing canonical on every page, parameters allowed to be crawled, sitemap lists clean URLs only.
  • Lowercase naming convention and a fixed list of mediums, with no personal data in URLs.
  • Shared tracking sheet generating the final links.
  • Short links and redirects tested for parameter loss.
  • First-touch UTM values saved into signup form fields, consent handled.
  • Monthly check for Unassigned traffic and indexed utm URLs.
