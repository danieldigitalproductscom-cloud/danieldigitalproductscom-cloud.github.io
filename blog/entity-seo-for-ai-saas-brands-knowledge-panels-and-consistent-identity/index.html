Entity SEO for AI SaaS Brands: Get Search Engines and AI Assistants to Recognize Who You Are

Search engines and AI assistants do not only match keywords; they try to work out which real-world things a page is about. If your product name is also a common word, a person's name or another company's brand, you will be confused with them. Entity SEO is the work of making it unambiguous who you are, what you sell and where your official presence lives. For a young AI product with a generic name, it is one of the cheapest ways to improve branded search and the accuracy of what assistants say about you.

Step 1: Write one canonical description of the company

Decide on a single factual sentence: what the product is, who it is for, and the company name. For example: "Acme Ledger is accounting software for freelancers, made by Acme Labs." Use it, with only light variation, in your homepage hero or intro, About page, meta description, social bios, directory listings and press boilerplate. Consistent wording gives machines repeated, matching evidence. Avoid slogans in these places; a tagline tells a crawler nothing about category.

Step 2: Fix naming and profile consistency

List every place the brand appears and make the basics identical:

  • Name. Pick one spelling and capitalization ("Acme Ledger", not "AcmeLedger" in some places). Decide whether the legal entity name is shown anywhere and keep it consistent.
  • Profiles. LinkedIn company page, GitHub organization, X or Bluesky, YouTube, Crunchbase, Product Hunt, G2 or Capterra, and app marketplaces if relevant.
  • Details. Same logo, same website URL (with or without www, matching your canonical), same founding year, same description, same support email domain.

Claim profiles you do not control yet. Unclaimed or outdated listings are a common source of wrong facts in AI answers.

Step 3: Add Organization structured data

On your homepage, or the About page, add JSON-LD describing the organization. A minimal version:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "Acme Labs",
  "url": "https://www.example.com/",
  "logo": "https://www.example.com/logo.png",
  "description": "Acme Labs makes Acme Ledger, accounting software for freelancers.",
  "foundingDate": "2024",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.linkedin.com/company/example",
    "https://github.com/example",
    "https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/example"
  ]
}

The sameAs array is the important part: it points to profiles you control or that are about you, linking the pieces together. Only list URLs that are really about your organization, and make sure each of those profiles links back to your site. If you have a separate product, consider a SoftwareApplication block on the product page with the same naming. Validate with the Rich Results Test or the Schema Markup Validator, and keep the markup matching visible page content.

Step 4: Make the About page do real work

Give the About page the facts an entity needs: company name, what it does, when it was founded, where it is based, who the founders are (with links to their profiles), and how to contact you. Link to press coverage, talks and your Organization profiles. Founder pages with a clear bio and links to the same people's public profiles help connect people and company, which is useful when the product is new and has little other evidence.

Step 5: Consider Wikidata, and be careful with Wikipedia

Wikidata is a structured database anyone can edit, and it is a common reference for knowledge systems. An item for your company is reasonable if you can cite independent sources, such as news coverage or filings, for its statements. Follow its notability and referencing policies, avoid promotional edits, and disclose any conflict of interest if you edit about your own business. Wikipedia has stricter notability rules: a new startup rarely qualifies, and creating a page yourself usually gets it deleted. If you do not meet the bar, skip it and build independent coverage instead.

Step 6: Build third-party mentions that name you correctly

Entities are confirmed by what others say about them. Aim for mentions that state your name and category together: a podcast description, a directory listing, a customer case study, a conference speaker page, an integration partner's page. Ask partners to use your exact name and a link to the canonical URL. Check for unlinked mentions and incorrect descriptions periodically, and ask the publisher for a correction when facts are wrong.

Step 7: Handle name collisions

If a bigger brand or a common word shares your name, pair the name with the category in titles and headings: "Acme Ledger accounting software" in the homepage title, for example. Use the category consistently in link anchors you control. Check how branded queries look: search the name alone and with the category, and note what appears. If the results show a different company, you need more category-plus-name signals, not more keywords.

Step 8: Check what search and assistants say about you

Every month, run a small test set: your brand name, "what is [brand]", "[brand] pricing", "[brand] vs [competitor]" in Google and in the AI assistants your buyers use. Record wrong facts, such as outdated pricing, a discontinued feature or a confused founder. Trace each one to its likely source, usually an old profile, a directory page or your own stale copy, and fix it there. Updating the source is more effective than asking for a one-off correction.

Quick checklist

  1. One canonical company description, reused across properties.
  2. Identical name, logo, URL and description on all profiles, all claimed.
  3. Organization JSON-LD with an accurate sameAs list, validated.
  4. About page with founders, dates, location and contact details.
  5. Wikidata item only if independent sources support it; no self-written Wikipedia page.
  6. Third-party mentions that use your exact name and category.
  7. Monthly branded-query and assistant check, with fixes made at the source.

Entity work pays off slowly and shows up as cleaner branded results, fewer wrong answers about your product and better click-through on name searches.
