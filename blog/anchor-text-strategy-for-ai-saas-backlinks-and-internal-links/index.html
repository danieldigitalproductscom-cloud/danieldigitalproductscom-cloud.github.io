Anchor Text Strategy for AI SaaS: How to Word Backlinks and Internal Links Without Looking Spammy

Anchor text is the clickable wording of a link. Search engines use it as a short description of the page being linked to, which is why it matters. It is also why careless handling is risky: years of spam updates have taught Google to distrust profiles where every link says exactly "best AI writing tool". For an AI SaaS site the practical goal is simple. Let external anchors look like what real people write, and make internal anchors as descriptive as you like, because you control them.

1. Know the anchor types

  • Branded: "Acme", "acme.ai", "the Acme team". The safest and usually the largest share.
  • Naked URL: "https://acme.ai/pricing". Common in citations, newsletters and directories.
  • Exact match: "AI meeting notes software". Helpful in small doses, a red flag in bulk.
  • Partial match / descriptive: "this tool for turning calls into action items". Natural and informative.
  • Generic: "click here", "this article", "source". Weak for relevance but normal in any real profile.
  • Image links: the alt text acts as the anchor, so write meaningful alt text on logos and screenshots.

2. Aim for the profile a real brand has, not a target ratio

There is no official ideal percentage, and nobody outside Google knows one. What you can observe is that established products tend to have a profile dominated by brand names and URLs, with a long tail of varied descriptive phrases and only a small slice of exact-match commercial terms. If you open a backlink tool and see 40 percent of links using the same money keyword, that is unusual, and it is typically a sign of paid placements, aggressive guest-post deals or a past agency. A young AI product with mostly branded anchors is not under-optimized. It is normal.

3. Stop requesting specific anchors

The fastest way to build a spammy profile is to ask every partner, journalist and guest-post host to use your exact keyword phrase. Instead:

  • Ask for a link to the right page, and leave the wording to the writer.
  • When you supply a boilerplate description for directories and partner pages, vary it across sources and lead with your brand name.
  • For integration and marketplace listings, a branded anchor is fine. The context already tells search engines what you do.
  • Put effort into the surrounding text. A link inside a sentence about AI contract review, on a page about legal tech, carries more topical signal than any anchor tweak.

4. Make internal anchors descriptive and varied

Internal links are the one place where descriptive anchors are low risk, because they are expected to describe the destination. Good habits:

  • Use words that say what the reader will find: "usage-based pricing examples", not "read more".
  • Link to your cornerstone pages (pricing, a feature page, a use-case page) from relevant blog posts with the topic in the anchor.
  • Vary the wording across posts. Ten links to the same page can use five phrasings that all describe it accurately.
  • Keep anchors short enough to scan, usually two to six words. A whole sentence as a link is hard to read and dilutes the signal.
  • Avoid identical anchors pointing at different pages. That muddies which page should rank, a cause of keyword cannibalization.

5. Watch navigation and template anchors

Sitewide links in headers, footers and sidebars repeat on every page. A footer link that says "AI Chatbot Software for Customer Service Teams" on 2,000 URLs is an over-optimized pattern you created yourself. Keep nav labels plain ("Pricing", "Customer support") and let in-body links do the descriptive work. Likewise, check programmatically generated "related posts" modules so they use the post title rather than a stuffed keyword.

6. Write link-worthy sentences when you contribute elsewhere

When you author a guest article, podcast show notes or a data study, the anchor falls out of the sentence. Say "our benchmark of 40 transcription models" and link those words. That reads like a citation, because it is one. Avoid dropping a lone keyword into a sentence that does not otherwise need it. Editors notice, and so do algorithms.

7. Audit what you already have

  1. Export referring-link anchors from Search Console (Links report, top linking text) and your backlink tool of choice.
  2. Group them into the types above and look for a single commercial phrase far above everything else.
  3. For unnatural patterns tied to a source you can reach, ask the site owner to switch to your brand name or remove the link. Disavow is a last resort and only for clearly manipulative links you cannot get removed.
  4. Crawl your own site and list internal anchors per target URL. Look for "click here" pointing at key pages, and for different pages that share the same anchor.
  5. Fix the highest-value pages first: pricing, product and top-converting use-case pages.

8. Do not forget accessibility

Descriptive anchors also help screen reader users, who often navigate by a list of links. A list that reads "Learn more, Learn more, Learn more" is useless. If a design needs a short visible label, add an accessible name such as aria-label or visually hidden text that says what the link does.

Quick checklist

  • External anchors are mostly branded or URL, with natural variety.
  • You no longer request exact-match keyword anchors from partners or hosts.
  • Internal links describe the destination in two to six words.
  • No two different pages compete for the same internal anchor.
  • Footer, header and template links use plain, short labels.
  • Image links have useful alt text.
  • You review the anchor profile once a quarter and after any link campaign.

Anchor text is a refinement, not a growth lever on its own. Relevant pages, honest citations and a site structure that is easy to crawl do most of the work.
