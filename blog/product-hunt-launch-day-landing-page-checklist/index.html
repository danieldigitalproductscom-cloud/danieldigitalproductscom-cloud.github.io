The Product Hunt Launch Day Checklist for Your AI/SaaS Landing Page

Most founders spend weeks preparing their Product Hunt post — the tagline, the maker comment, the gallery images — and almost no time preparing the page that traffic actually lands on. That's backwards. A hunter clicks through with less patience and less context than any other visitor you'll get all year: they've already skimmed a card with your name, tagline, and a screenshot, and they're deciding in about five seconds whether to keep reading or hit back and scroll to the next launch. Here's what to fix on your landing page before launch day, not during it.

1. Match the page to the card, word for word

If your Product Hunt tagline says "Turn meeting notes into action items automatically" and your hero headline says something else, you've introduced a half-second of confusion at the exact moment you can least afford it. Copy the tagline language straight onto the page. Consistency between the card someone just read and the headline they land on is a bigger conversion lever than clever copywriting.

2. Assume they'll never scroll past the fold

Product Hunt visitors are the least patient traffic you'll get. Your hero needs the what, the who-it's-for, and a single clear action, all visible without scrolling. If your "how it works" or pricing only makes sense three sections down, most of this traffic never sees it. This is the day to temporarily tighten, not the day to add more sections.

3. Build for mobile first — most of the click-throughs will be

A large share of Product Hunt browsing now happens on phones, especially in the first few hours when the community is checking rankings between other things. Load your page on an actual phone the week before launch, not just in a responsive-mode browser tab. Check that your primary button is reachable with a thumb without zooming, and that nothing important is hidden behind a hover state that doesn't exist on touch.

4. Add a launch-day banner, and make it removable

A thin, dismissible bar — "🚀 We're live on Product Hunt today — support us here" linking to your PH page — does two things: it gives your own traffic (people who see your tweet or newsletter) a fast path to go upvote, and it signals momentum to organic visitors who land during the launch window. Build it so a single config flag turns it off the next day; a stale "we're live today" banner from three weeks ago is a worse trust signal than no banner at all.

5. Tag your links so you can actually measure the day

Add UTM parameters to the link in your Product Hunt post (?utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=launch) before you submit it, not after someone points out you forgot. Without it, your analytics will lump PH traffic in with everything else, and you'll have no way to answer the question every founder asks the next morning: did that actually convert, or just spike a number?

6. Make sure the page survives a traffic spike you can't predict

A front-page Product Hunt launch can send more visitors in six hours than your site normally sees in a month. If your page is a static HTML/CSS build with no server-rendered backend in the request path, this is mostly a non-issue — which is exactly why so many launch pages are built that way on purpose. If you're on a platform with cold starts or database-backed rendering, test it under load beforehand or put a CDN in front of it. A page that goes slow or down during your one concentrated traffic day is the single worst-timed outage you can have.

7. Put a real human behind live chat for the day

If you have any kind of chat widget, staff it — even just you — for the hours your launch is live. Product Hunt commenters and early visitors ask sharper, more skeptical questions than average traffic, and a fast, honest answer in chat converts better than anything on the page itself. If you don't normally run live chat, this is a fine day to turn it on temporarily and off again afterward.

8. Refresh your social proof the morning of, not the week before

If your hero shows a user count, a review score, or a "trusted by" logo strip, update it the morning of launch so it reflects reality, not last month. Product Hunt is a community that fact-checks; a stale number a commenter notices and calls out publicly does more damage than an honest, smaller number would have.

9. Give newly-arrived signups an easy way to say where they came from

A one-line note in your welcome email or onboarding flow — "Come from Product Hunt? Reply and let us know what you're hoping to use this for" — turns launch-day signups into a source of real qualitative feedback, and gives you a natural opening to ask happy new users for a review or comment on your post while the launch is still live.

10. Plan the day-after, not just the day-of

Whatever badge, ranking, or "#3 Product of the Day" result you get is worth adding to the page permanently once it's final — a Product Hunt badge is a real, evergreen trust signal for the next six months of organic traffic, not just launch-day decoration. Swap the temporary launch banner for the permanent badge once the day ends, and keep it.

The pattern underneath all of this

Product Hunt doesn't need a different landing page — it needs your existing page to work harder under worse conditions: less patience, more mobile, one concentrated day, and a skeptical, plugged-in audience. Fix the fold, fix the mobile experience, tag your links, and staff the chat. Everything else is decoration.

If you're prepping a launch page from scratch, starting from a template that's already fast, mobile-first, and structured around a single clear hero action means launch week is spent on messaging and outreach — not last-minute layout fixes.
